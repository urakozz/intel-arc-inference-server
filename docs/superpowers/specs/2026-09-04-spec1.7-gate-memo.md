The operator's own stopping criterion is met and the spec's efficiency bar is
not. Both statements are load-bearing; docs/BENCHMARKS carries them together
and so does docs/05's superseding block.

## 2. What the 3.9% lead actually is

Our end-to-end lead exists because our `lm_head` is int4 (`W` = 13.673
GB/token) and **vLLM cannot load a quantized `lm_head` at all** (measured,
docs/14) - it reads 15.540 GB. On the byte-matched `Vishva007` row, same
binary and same hour, vLLM leads 31.01 to **29.33**. The margin is a
checkpoint advantage we can take and they cannot; it is not evidence that our
kernels are better. Their KV was `fp8` against our `bf16`, which runs the
other way and is not quantified here.

## 4. Six cost models died here, and that is the transferable result

1. The **load-message model** for GEMV: removing 41% of load messages on
   byte-identical traffic moved the plateau shapes −0.4…+1.8%.
2. The **attention message-width** axis: 2D-block KV reads −8.9% against
   `vec2`'s −10.4% at 8× the messages. Prior art predicted −25%.
3. `hotkv2`'s **0.579 ms "ceiling for every locality attack"** - correct only
   for substitutions that hold the message count; register-packed reuse
   removes messages and beat it by 1.075.
4. docs/15's **"is the ~3.1 ms recoverable - no"** - falsified by that same
   1.075.
5. The **barrier price model**: `nobar` priced barriers at 0.242 µs each by
   deleting all six at once; the one redundant fence measured 0.096 µs
   marginal, 39% of prediction, because it sat two instructions before a
   surviving fence.
6. The **spill worry** on register packing: `zeinfo` shows no
   `spill_mem_size` at all (`private_size: 1152` is new and its traffic is
   unmeasured).

Pre-registration is what makes these findings rather than embarrassments: each
prediction was written down, then measured, then scored - including the one
that **missed high** (Task 5 predicted 1.000-1.065 and delivered 1.075).

## 5. What is left, and why nothing is queued behind it

At 74.7% MBU roughly a quarter of the card's bandwidth is not becoming tokens,
and **this spec ends with no priced lever for it**. The load-path axis is
falsified in both families, fusion is structurally unavailable, and Task 5's
own floor check is decisive: `attn_decode` with **all K/V global loads
removed** measured 92.4 µs/launch, while reaching the next tier needs ~87 µs -
i.e. below the no-loads row while still paying six softmax/reduction passes.
Anything further requires a **different decomposition** (SYCL-TLA / DPAS-style
parallel-in-block attention that collapses those passes), not another variant
of the current one.

Unpriced and out of scope this spec: overlap / second queue (Task 7, unrun);
fp8 KV (quality axis - would halve our KV traffic, vLLM already takes it);
MTP / speculative decode, where vLLM measures **46.13 t/s** on this box and is
the single largest unexploited factor in the comparison.

3. **Task 3** (docs/15 §1.7 ladder assembly) and **Task 7** (overlap probe)

4. `private_size: 1152` on `attn_decode` - unit and traffic cost unmeasured;

5. vLLM rows in docs/BENCHMARKS now span **three** builds under two tags
   (dev365, dev391, dev396). Each row carries its own version; none is
   re-measured across builds.
