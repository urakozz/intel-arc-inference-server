# Spec 1.5 - the re-assessment memo

Written 2026-08-25 at the spec's own §6 gate, on the **short path**. Spec 1.5
[§2](2026-08-25-spec1.5-decode-performance-design.md) set one success bar -
tg256 @ depth 4096, median of three, **≥ 31.50 t/s** - and one stopping rule:
work the lever ladder, run the gate, and **if short, stop and write this
document**. The ladder is spent, the gate is short, and nothing past this memo
starts without a decision on it.

This is not a proposal. It is the ledger of what was measured, the arithmetic
of what is left, and a priced menu. Every number below says whether it is
**measured**, **derived** (arithmetic over measurements) or **estimated** (a
model). Nothing here is new measurement: the gate bench is new, everything else
is consolidated from [15-step-anatomy.md](../../15-step-anatomy.md),
[12-kernels.md](../../12-kernels.md) and [14-golden-gate.md](../../14-golden-gate.md).

## 1. The verdict

| | t/s | ms/token | kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| the bar (vLLM `p314-t214-vxkp0`, no speculation) | **31.50** | **31.746** | measured, [BENCHMARKS.md](../../BENCHMARKS.md) |
| **the gate, `ef6acb0`** | **27.54** | **36.32** | **measured**, median of three, idle box, 2026-08-25 |
| spec 1.5's start (`62bdd4d`, phase 1) | 23.73 | 42.141 | measured |
| the roofline (15.540 GB/token ÷ 590 GB/s) | 37.97 | 26.34 | measured / derived |

**Short by 3.96 t/s - 12.6%, a factor of 1.144 the wrong way.** The remaining
gap is **4.574 ms/token** (36.32 − 31.746, derived).

The ladder moved the engine **42.141 → 36.32 ms/token, +16.1% throughput**
(23.73 → 27.54 t/s, derived), with the golden gate at **96/96 tokens
element-exact at every step** and the full suite green throughout. It closed
**56% of the 10.395 ms/token gap** the spec opened with (5.821 of it) - 49%
of the same distance measured in t/s, 3.81 of 7.77, because t/s is the
reciprocal - and it did not close the gate.

**This outcome was predicted, in writing, before the first lever was cut.**
docs/15's ranking section said "the ladder as specified is arithmetically
unlikely to clear the gate" and put its optimistic total at 34.4 ms/token; the
measured answer is 36.32 with two negligible levers unspent. The spec
anticipated it too ("the win is *arithmetically* reachable but tight") and
provided this document as the outcome. Nobody should be surprised here - and
nobody should read the shortfall as an argument that the ladder was the wrong
work. What the ladder produced, and what §5 below is priced from, is the first
per-launch cost model of this step that has survived contact with four
falsifications.

### 2.3 What the ladder proved that is worth more than the milliseconds

- **The mechanism named in advance was wrong every single time; the
  measurement was right every single time.** L2: the work-group count was worth
  zero and a K-split was everything. L1: the fold was priced and the rescale,
  treated as a formality, was 40% of the win. L5: the retile paid, the model
  that predicted it was falsified by +38%, its replacement by −13.8%, and the
  block size that shipped was 64 where the plan scoped 128.
- **Four cost models have now died on `attn_decode`** and this project has
  declined to write a fifth (docs/15 §L5). What dominates that kernel's
  current 224 µs launch is **unknown**.
- **Kernel count is not a first-class cost.** L1 *added* 129 launches to buy
  2.409 ms - a 25.5:1 trade at the derived 0.733 µs/launch. The whole 774-launch
  dispatch total is ~0.57 ms, 1.6% of the step (derived).
- **The subgroup, not the work-group, is the unit of parallelism on this
  device** - measured at 8, 16, 32, 128 and 320 subgroups across two kernels,
  sub-linear every time.

## 3. Where the 36.32 ms actually is

No lever touched GEMV. It was measured in situ once, at **29.005 ms**
(`43bb720`, 257 launches, +2.3% over the transplanted probe floor), and its
control row `lm_head` read within **+0.2%** across all three lever runs - so
29.005 is still the best figure for today's step (derived, cross-run, ±~0.15).

| part | ms/token | share of 36.32 | kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GEMV** - int4 mixers/MLPs (256) + bf16 `lm_head` (1) | **29.005** | **79.9%** | measured in situ (`43bb720`), untouched by every lever |
| - of which **`lm_head` alone** | **4.376** | 12.0% | measured in situ, 581 GB/s = 98.5% of the device |
| `attn_decode` + `attn_reduce` + `attn_prep` (48) | **3.839** | 10.6% | measured in situ (`c746840`) |
| `prep` family (370) | 1.202 | 3.3% | measured in situ (`b045e11`) |
| `gdn_step` (48) | 0.762 | 2.1% | measured in situ (`c746840`) |
| `a‖b` GEMV (48) | 0.256 | 0.7% | measured in situ (`b045e11`) |
| `embed_gather` + `argmax` (3) | 0.017 | 0.05% | measured in situ |
| dispatch gap, un-instrumented, 774 launches | 0.567 | 1.6% | derived (0.733 µs/launch) |
| host, outside the fence | 0.101 | 0.3% | measured (this gate's own runs) |
| **Σ** | **35.75** | | |

**The partition no longer closes, and by how much is itself a finding.** The
launches above are quoted from the runs that measured them, which is why the Σ
is 0.57 ms under the bench step; taking the *same* run's total instead - the
L5 after-run's Σ over all 774 launches, **35.342 ms measured** - plus the same
dispatch and host terms gives **36.010 ms against a measured 36.32**, i.e. the
step is **0.31 ms larger than the instrument can account for**. That is the same
0.31 ms §L5 recorded as unattributed. Two independent routes to the same
residual is what makes it a finding rather than rounding, and §5.4 is the item
that would settle it.

**Two consequences that decide everything in §5.**

1. **GEMV alone is 91.4% of the entire 31.746 ms budget** (29.005 / 31.746,
   derived). Everything that is not a GEMV has to fit in **2.741 ms**; it costs
   **~7.3 ms** today. There is no arrangement of the remaining non-GEMV work
   that clears the bar - the bucket the whole spec attacked is now too small to
   pay for the gap.
2. **The only lever left that is large enough to matter is inside GEMV**, and
   the only GEMV item that is not already at 90-99% of device bandwidth is
   `lm_head`'s dtype.

## 5. The menu, priced

Each item: expected yield (with its kind), correctness risk, **which proofs it
re-opens**, and cost. Spec §6 names the redesigns it wanted priced; every one of
them is here, including the two the measurement now argues *against*.

### 5.1 Quantise `lm_head` to int4 - the headline, and not a closer

- **Yield: ~3.3 ms/token (estimated).** `lm_head` is **4.376 ms measured in
  situ**, one launch, 2.543 GB of the 15.540 GB token; at int4 g64 the same row
  is 0.66 GB and docs/12's bandwidth arithmetic puts it at **~1.1 ms
  (estimated)**. Nothing about the kernel changes - it is already at 581 GB/s,
  98.5% of the measured device bandwidth. This is a **bytes** lever, not a
  kernel lever, and it is the only one of that kind left.
- **It moves the roofline too** (derived): W drops 15.540 → 13.657 GB/token, so
  the ceiling rises **37.97 → 43.2 t/s**. Note the direction this pushes MBU:
  at 32.66 ms the engine would read 418 GB/s = **70.9%** of the device, *below*
  today's 72.5%. Quantising `lm_head` removes the most efficient work in the
  step; what is left is the inefficient part. That is the honest framing, and
  it is an argument for doing the probe (§5.2) either way.
- **Correctness risk: the highest of any item here, and structurally so.**
  `lm_head` is the last matmul before `argmax`. The golden gate's whole
  assertion is that 96 `argmax` decisions land on the same ids as a CPU oracle,
  and today's logits cosines are 0.999844 … 0.999989 - the tightest margin in
  the chain sits exactly where this change lands. Plan-3 evidence says the slack
  is large (a 21% hidden-state deviation left tokens exact, docs/14), but a
  flipped token here would be a *legitimate* flip, not a bug, and spec §2's
  ruling then applies: **stop and surface it as a decision**, never absorb it.
- **Proofs re-opened:**
  1. **The checkpoint requant path** - the shipped checkpoint has `lm_head`
     bf16 row-major `[N][K]`. Producing int4 means either an AutoRound run over
     that tensor or an offline requant tool in this repo (RTN g64 to the
     loader's own convention). Either way it is **new numerics that the oracle
     must agree with**, and spec 1.5 §9 put it explicitly out of scope.
  2. **The loader's int4 path for this shape** - 5120×248320 is 5× the largest
     int4 tensor the packing asserts have ever seen; docs/13's invariant checks,
     the repack and the `W` cross-check all re-run.
  3. **The dequant fixture** - `tools/oracle/dequant.py` is the bit-compare
     convention the C++ loader is graded against; a new quantisation is only
     trustworthy once that comparison covers it.
  4. **The full golden re-proof**, 96/96, all three prompts - and note it is no
     longer a re-run of an unchanged comparison: the vLLM leg of docs/14's
     three-way chain runs a **bf16** `lm_head`, so that leg stops being an
     apples-to-apples check the moment ours is int4.
  5. Doc 07 #6 ("how much accuracy does quantising `lm_head` cost?") becomes
     load-bearing instead of open.
- **Cost:** days, not hours, and most of it is in the requant + proof, not the
  engine. **It is the largest measured item left in the step and it does not
  clear the bar on its own.**

### 5.2 A probe that names `attn_decode`'s 224 µs - cheapest information here

- **Yield: none directly. Information only** - and docs/15's own verdict is
  that "a probe that identifies that term is now worth more than any design
  written against the models that have already died."
- **Why it is first in value order.** `attn_decode` is **3.585 ms measured,
  9.9% of the step** and the largest non-GEMV row by 4.7×. Four cost models
  have been falsified on it (two depth fits, two block fits). What is *measured*
  and not fitted: work-group count is free from 20 to 260 work-groups; depth
  64 → 1024 costs +120% while 1024 → 4096 costs +6.9%; each block halving cost
  ~73 µs until the fourth, which cost 20. Model D called ~223 µs of the
  *original* 370 "not the block walk" - and the launch is now 224 µs. **The
  kernel is now essentially all of the term nobody has named.**
- **Shape:** a `probe_attn` in the mould of `probe_gemv` - transplanted
  launches, one variable at a time: the 6× q-head reread, the softmax pass, the
  KV read pattern, `head_dim`, the fixed per-launch floor. It changes no shipped
  kernel.
- **Correctness risk: none.** No shipped code path moves.
- **Proofs re-opened: none.**
- **Cost: about a day.** If it names a term worth ≥1 ms, it is the second item
  §4 says the project cannot currently name. If it names one worth 0.2 ms, that
  is a decisive answer too, and it costs a day to get.

### 5.3 A deep-context golden prompt - a trust item, and a precondition

- **Yield: none in ms.** This is the standing correctness gap L5 opened.
- **The gap, ruled in docs/15 §L5 and recorded in docs/14:** the golden gate's
  longest prompt reaches `pos` **92**. At `ATTN_BLOCK` 64 that is two blocks;
  the benchmark runs **65**. The gate therefore covers the block-merge
  reassociation barely and the depth-4096 merge **not at all**. For spec 1.5 the
  ruling was that `attn_test`'s ulp bars (worst measured: **1 bf16 ulp on one
  word** against a ruled bar of 2, at `pos` 4095 and 16383) **are** the
  deep-depth authority. That was tolerable while attention was untouched. A
  lever has now reassociated it, and the next attention change (§5.2 → anything,
  or §5.6) has no oracle at the depth it is benchmarked at.
- **Cost, priced honestly from the oracle's own measured numbers** (tools/oracle
  README, measured 2026-08-24): today one prompt is 118 s load+dequant + ~0.22
  s/id prefill (derived from the 38-id and 61-id points) + **5.8 s/token**
  decode, 61.4 GiB peak RSS, ~300 MiB of output.
  - At **T ≈ 4096** the prefill extrapolates to **~15 minutes (estimated, and a
    lower bound** - the extrapolation is linear between two points 23 ids apart
    and attention is quadratic in T); 32 greedy tokens is ≥3 minutes on top.
    Call it **20-30 minutes of oracle wall per prompt**, which is *not* the
    blocker.
  - **The blockers are the fixture and the RSS.** The manifest's 192 residual /
    mixer / mlp taps are `[T, 5120]` and the logits are `[T+32, 248320]`; at
    T = 4096 that is **~20 GB per prompt (estimated)** against 300 MiB today, on
    top of a 61.4 GiB peak on a 121 GB box. **The fixture has to be trimmed**
    (ids + logits at the decision points, or sub-sampled taps) before this is
    runnable at all - that is a real design decision, not a longer run.
  - **And it lands on the test, not just the oracle:** the engine ingests at
    **34.43 ms/token measured**, so a 4096-id prompt costs the gate ~141 s of
    ingest *per prompt* - a test that runs in seconds today becomes minutes.
- **Proofs re-opened / closed:** it *closes* the deep-depth attention gap and
  it re-opens the golden fixture format and the gate's own runtime budget.
- **Ruling to carry forward:** any further attention work - including §5.6 -
  should be gated behind this, because the gate as it stands cannot see the
  class of change attention work makes.

### 5.4 Instrument upgrade: repeated-run averaging

- **Yield: it unlocks attribution below 0.3 ms**, which is where everything
  left on the shelf lives - including **L4's 0.659 ms of measured in-situ GEMV
  excess**, currently un-attackable because each candidate is worth less than
  the drift.
- **The measured problem.** The profiler reproduces to **0.006%** over two runs
  three minutes apart, but across a day of work on the box the *untouched*
  launches drift **+0.30% / +0.50% / +0.56%** - 0.12 to 0.18 ms per comparison.
  Two levers in a row then missed their in-situ→bench prediction by more than
  the ±0.1 ms this method claims: L1 by 0.087 (inside, barely), **L5 by 0.32
  (outside, and unattributed)**. §3 above reaches the same 0.31 ms from a
  different direction. A residual the instrument cannot explain is a finding
  about the instrument.
- **Shape:** N whole-process repetitions with per-family mean and spread, or
  interleaved A/B of the two binaries within one session so drift cancels
  instead of accumulating. Both are script-and-CLI work.
- **Correctness risk: none. Proofs re-opened: none. Cost: hours.**
- It is the *precondition* for believing any yield under ~0.5 ms, which is
  every remaining item except §5.1 and possibly §5.2.

### 5.5 The remaining floors - small, and mostly argued against

| item | measured today | its own traffic floor | remaining | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `a‖b` GEMV | 0.256 ms (5.34 µs/launch) | 0.107 ms (2.22 µs) | **≤0.15 ms** | **finished.** A 32-way split reorders the sum and needs its own golden run; it cannot repay it |
| `prep_res_fold` + `prep_norm_finish` | 0.484 ms | 0.085 ms (5.7×) | ~0.40 ms | stage B's 20-deep `sumsq` chain and its `resid` re-read. Neither repays a golden run alone |
| the whole `prep` family (370 launches) | 1.202 ms | 0.154 ms (7.8×) | ~1.05 ms | the only sub-GEMV row with a *ms* of headroom, and no design exists for it that is not fusion (§5.6) |
| `gdn_step` | 0.762 ms | 0.671 ms (1.09×) | **≤0.06 ms** | exonerated, permanently |
| GEMV `S` retune (L4) | 0.659 ms of excess over the probe floor | - | ≤0.3 ms | blocked on §5.4; each candidate costs a golden run |

**SLM-staged attention - the plan's own L5 design, never built, and now argued
against rather than deferred.** The `ATTN_BLOCK` sweep bounds it without any
cost model (which matters, since this kernel has killed four): halving the
block halves the positions one work-group walks serially, and the **second**
halving (64 → 32) bought only 20.2 µs/launch - so everything still *walk-shaped*
in a launch is roughly **0.3-0.6 ms/token** across the 16 layers (derived).
Staging attacks a **subset** of that - the 6× q-head reread *inside* the walk,
not the walk - for six accumulators and six `(mx, sm)` pairs per work-item and
**the highest correctness risk in the project** (it restructures the online
softmax). Its ceiling is a fraction of a fraction of a 3.585 ms row, and §5.3
says the gate cannot currently see the change. **No.**

### 5.6 The fusions, and the one idea that is dead

- **`norm` → GEMV prologue** (spec-1 §4.1 item 1, 258 launches). **Unpriced,
  and it should stay unpriced until §5.2 and §5.4 land.** Its ceiling is the
  whole `prep_res` pair, **≤0.484 ms**, of which only ~0.19 ms is dispatch
  (derived); the rest is the `x` round-trip. **The risk is not in the norm, it
  is in the GEMV:** the prologue lands inside the family that is **29.005 ms,
  79.9% of the step**, and this project has already measured what perturbing
  that codegen for an unrelated reason costs - the L2 `KU` variant moved
  `lm_head` by **−1.3%** while buying nothing. 1.3% of the GEMV family is
  **0.377 ms (derived)**, which is the fusion's entire prize. **Proofs
  re-opened:** every GEMV variant's reference comparison, the split-K table
  guards (`check_sizes` at capture), the bf16 rounding point, and the golden
  gate - for a prize the same size as its own risk.
- **`a‖b` → `gdn_step` prologue.** **Moot.** L2 took `a‖b` to 0.256 ms inside
  its own kernel, so absorbing it buys **≤0.256 ms** at best and costs a fused
  kernel. docs/04 already records item 4 of its fusion list as moot for this
  reason.
- **Context-bucketed command lists.** **Dead, measured twice.** A grid sized for
  `max_len` 16384 costs **0.046 ms/token** whole-step (doc 07 #12) and **0.04%**
  of `attn_decode` per kernel (docs/15 §2, two runs differing by 192 idle
  work-groups per layer). Spec §6 lists it; the answer is that it was already
  answered.
- **Layout changes.** No candidate. Every GEMV shape runs the probe's own best
  layout and `S`, at 90-99% of the measured 590 GB/s, and the one bf16 layout
  question (`lm_head` row-major vs the canonical tile) was decided when the
  kernel landed and is subsumed by §5.1.

## 7. What this memo asks for

Spec 1.5 **closes** here by its own stopping rule (§2: "when the levers are
exhausted … run the §6 gate. If short: stop"). The tag `spec1.5-done` marks the
short close; every record - README, docs/05, docs/12, docs/14, docs/15,
BENCHMARKS.md - carries the honest number.

The decision is what spec 1.6 is scoped from. Three shapes, in the order this
measurement supports:

1. **Information first (§5.2 + §5.4, ~1-2 days, zero correctness surface).**
   The probe that names `attn_decode`'s 224 µs, and repeated-run averaging so
   sub-0.3 ms yields become believable. This is what docs/15 says is worth more
   than any further design, and it is the only path that could *produce* the
   second item §4 says does not currently exist.
2. **Bytes (§5.1, days, the project's largest correctness surface).** `lm_head`
   at int4 is worth ~3.3 ms and **still misses the bar by 0.88 t/s on its own**.
   Taking it without item 1 means spending the requant + full re-proof and
   still not clearing 31.50.
3. **Trust (§5.3).** The deep-context golden prompt. Not a performance item,
   and a precondition for any further attention work.

**The recommendation this measurement supports is 1, then 3, then 2** - because
2 is the only item whose price is known and whose *sufficiency* is known to be
negative, and because 1 is the only item that can change that.

What is **not** recommended, on measured grounds: SLM-staged attention (§5.5),
`norm`→GEMV fusion before the probe (§5.6), `a‖b`→`gdn_step` fusion (moot),
context-bucketed lists (dead), and L3/L4 as ladder items (ruled, §2.2).
