## Review Focus

1. **The sandwich, twice a layer.** The post norms normalise the sub-block's OWN bf16 output (o_proj's row; down's row) with eps 1e-8 and `(1 + w)`, then add to the residual with one rounding, then reduce the new residual's Σ² for the next centered norm (eps 1e-5). Kolibri's chain had PLAIN `w` with two roundings (`kol_norm_elem`); Muse's is `(1 + w)` in fp32 with ONE rounding (M:122-136). A twin test fails if either norm uses Kolibri's form.
2. **q's scale after the norm, before RoPE, with two roundings:** `q = bf16(bf16(q̂) x 3.87f)`; then RoPE (sliding only). k: `bf16(k̂)` then RoPE. The attention scale `0.0883883...` multiplies the score after the q·k sum (eager: `bf16(bf16(q·k) x s)`; flash: fp32, recorded deviation).
3. **The ring's addressing at the window's edge:** a sliding query at p sees exactly keys `p - 2047 .. p` (MU:93-102); ring row `k & 4095`. Tests at p = 2046, 2047, 2048, 4095, 4096, 4097, 9000 (the twin) and on the card.
4. **The gate's operand is the normed input** `xn` (the attention's input), not the residual: `a = bf16(attn x bf16(sigmoid(g)))` with `g` from the fused q||gate||k||v GEMV's rows 4096..8191.
5. **NoPE layers see every position** up to `max_len` and get no rotary: their K rows are position-free, so a full layer's output for a prompt shifted by s positions is identical (twin) - the engine must not apply the rotary table on `full[l]`.
