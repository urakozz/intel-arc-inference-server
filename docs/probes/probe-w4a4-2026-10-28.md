# Probe: W4A4 - int4 weights x int4 activations at group 256 (docs/07 §3)

**Verdict: no. W4A4 does not pay on the B70 for Qwen3.8, anywhere it would matter. Closed.**
Measured on the box CPU 2026-10-28 (runbook stages `r35.grid`, `r35.per_class`, both PASS, tree
0fb0cb9); written up 2026-11-07. The GPU microprobe (the plan's Task 3) did not run: the stopping rule
closed W4A4 on accuracy first, as intended.

## What was asked

Our prefill today is **W4A16** (int4 weights, bf16 activations - "activations" are the numbers flowing
between layers, the x in x W^T) with an int8 fast path, **h8**: a Hadamard rotation (a fixed orthogonal
mixing of the K dimension that spreads outlier values across many elements) on both operands, then int8
activations and int8 weights per channel. The B70's DPAS unit (the matrix-multiply instruction) also has
an int4 x int4 form, `i4_i4_matrix_mad_k64`, at **733.8 TIOP/s = 4.0x bf16, 2x int8**. W4A4 would use it:
both operands 4-bit. The question was whether 4-bit activations keep the output close enough to be worth a
kernel.

**Where the speed would come from (derived, unmeasured).** A GEMM over depth K costs K/16 bf16 DPAS
issues, or K/64 int4 x int4 issues plus one fp32 rescale per group along K (the two operands' scales
change every group). Fitting W4A8's four measured speeds gives ~1.6 issues a rescale, ~0.875 of peak; at
group 256 that projects **~2.5x bf16 per linear, ~1.4x over h8's ~1.75x ceiling** (h8 measured 1.35-1.52x
per linear, 1.28x on the whole prefill). Decode would not change: it is memory-bound and reads the same
int4 weights either way.

## What was measured

`tools/rotate/eval_quantised.py sim` on the box CPU (oracle container): the bf16 `Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B` as the
reference, the int4 g64 gate checkpoint `urakozz/Qwen3.8-27B-W4A16-g64-AutoRound-GPTQ` (snapshot 84575a1)
as the weights, every decoder linear's forward replaced by the variant (fp32 inside), logits compared
position by position against bf16. Variants, all on the same load and the same prompts:

| variant | what it computes |
|---|---|
| `none` | x W^T in fp32 with the checkpoint's int4 g64 weights - the weight-only floor every row includes |
| `w4a16` | the shipped model's own bf16 forward (what we serve today) |
| `h8` | today's int8 path: Q8(xR) Q8pc(WR)^T, R = the 1024-block Hadamard |
| `h4` | W4A4: h8's rotation exactly, then int4 symmetric on BOTH operands, one scale per 256 along K (groups aligned on both), q in [-8, 7], s = max/7.5 |
| `h4p2` | `h4` with every scale rounded up to a power of two (the cheap shift-rescale a kernel would want) |

**What the error columns measure.** *rel L2* is the size of the difference between the variant's logits
(the model's score for every vocabulary entry at every position) and bf16's, as a fraction of bf16's - one
number for "how far the whole output moved". *argmax* counts positions whose top next token is still
bf16's. *top-5* is the overlap of the five best tokens. *KL* is how far the predicted next-token probability
distribution drifts from bf16's (nats; 0 = identical). Lower rel L2 / KL and higher argmax / top-5 are better.

Prompts: `cjk` (38 ids), `code` (61), `heldout` (478, re-made from this tree's texts - not probe-w4a8
§15.4's file, so read it against this run's own controls), `long512` (the first 512 ids of
`tests/golden/prompts/long.ids`). The prompts' sha256 prefixes: cjk 76cc589f, code 9adc68ee, heldout
e24b3480, long512 f287eb13.

## Verdict, in plain words

**Does W4A4 pay on the B70? No.** The speed is real on paper (~1.4x over our int8 prefill, derived), but
4-bit activations roughly **double the model's output error**: on the 478-id held-out prompt the logits'
relative error goes from 12.5 % (h8) to 23.3 % (h4), and over all four prompts 3.4 fewer positions in
100 keep bf16's top next token than under h8. The int4 weights alone already cost ~12 %; h8 adds half a point on top; h4 adds eleven.

**Why.** An int4 number has 16 levels against int8's 256. Weights are static and AutoRound can tune them
onto 16 levels; activations change with every token and carry outliers (a few channels tens of times
larger than the rest). The Hadamard rotation spreads those outliers, which is what made int8 work (h8 is
within 0.5 points of the shipped model), but with 16 levels even a rotated group of 256 values is too
coarse: the largest value sets the scale and everything small rounds to 0 or +-1. Power-of-two scales make
it worse, because the scale can only round up, wasting up to half of the 16 levels.

**Where, if anywhere?** Only the attention output projection (`attn_o`) stays inside the rule on its own
(+1.08 / +0.63 points, argmax within 3 of 478). It is **2.1 %** of the prefill GEMM work; moving it from
h8 to W4A4 at the projected speeds would save about 0.6 % of the linears' time (derived) - not worth a
second GEMM kernel and a second weight format. `gdn_out` and `down` alone are near the line on long512 but
over it on heldout; the classes that carry the work (gate_up 47 %, down 23 %, gdn_in 17 %) are the worst
or near it.

**Caveats, and why they do not change the answer.** The `h4` weights are the g64 checkpoint re-rounded to
g256 by round-to-nearest, which understates what an AutoRound g256 checkpoint would give (plan Task 2 Step
2). That step was gated on `h4` being within ~3 points of h8; it is 7.7 to 13.6 points over, and the
activation half alone (16 levels on values that are not tuned) is enough to fail the rule, so a tuned
weight grid would not close the gap. Task 3's microprobe of the rescale cost was gated on a pass and did
not run, so the speed numbers above stay derived.

**What stays.** h8 (per-channel int8 activations after the rotation) remains the prefill fast path; int4
g64 W4A16 stays the one weight format. The `sim` tool keeps `none` / `a8` / `a8w8` / `h8` / `w4a16`,
`--per-class` and the unfiltered KL; the `h4` / `h4p2` variants, their tests and the row 35 stages are
removed.
